# B2B Marketing Excellence - The Bar We Write To

The mission: make 8-Thon the name people think of for AI implementation in
real businesses. Every piece of content either builds that authority or it
doesn't ship.

## How B2B buying actually works (write for it)

- Nobody impulse-buys B2B. A buying decision involves an owner or a small
  committee, a budget conversation, and weeks of quiet evaluation before
  anyone fills a form. Most of your future clients are watching silently
  right now.
- So: content compounds. The job of a post or video is rarely "convert
  today." It's "be the obvious choice when their AI problem gets loud."
- 95% of the market isn't buying this quarter. Write memory-building proof
  for them, and clear next steps for the 5% who are ready.
- Trust transfers from specifics. One real system walkthrough with real
  numbers outsells ten "AI will change everything" posts.

## Demand creation vs demand capture

- **Create demand** (most content): show problems owners didn't know were
  costing them money, show working systems, take positions. LinkedIn posts,
  YouTube videos, teardowns.
- **Capture demand** (a smaller share): direct offers, audits, "book a
  call" pieces aimed at people already looking. Never mix the two jobs in
  one piece.

## The AI-implementation video engine (our YouTube lane)

Video types that win this niche, in order of proof density:

1. **System build/walkthrough:** "Watch me build the automation that does X
   for a real business." Screen up, numbers visible, warts kept in.
2. **Teardown:** take a real SMB workflow (with permission or anonymized)
   and show exactly where AI removes hours. Before-state, after-state,
   dollars.
3. **Honest tool test:** "I ran 7 AI tools on a real lead list. Five
   padded their numbers." Testing beats reviewing. Show receipts.
4. **Case story:** client project from problem to result, told through the
   client's Tuesday, not our deliverables list.
5. **Contrarian position:** "Most SMBs should NOT buy AI tools yet." Takes
   earn subscribers; hedges don't.

Rules for the lane: every video anchored to a real system or real numbers;
titles promise the business outcome, not the technology; one video = one
workflow; CTA routes to the next proof, not to five links.

## LinkedIn x YouTube flywheel

- Every video yields 2-3 LinkedIn posts: the sharpest claim, the surprising
  number, the lesson learned.
- LinkedIn tests the hooks; the winners become video titles.
- Comments and DMs are the research lab: what owners actually ask becomes
  next month's content.

## The standard for "best"

Before anything ships, it passes this bar:

1. Could only WE have written this? (Uses our systems, our numbers, our
   clients' reality. If any agency could post it, kill it.)
2. Would a skeptical owner screenshot this for a partner? (Useful enough to
   share inside a business, not just like.)
3. Is there one specific, verifiable proof point? (A number, a named
   system, a real run.)
4. Does it sound like a person who builds, not a brand that markets?
